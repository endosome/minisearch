# WP-00 baseline: pinned build, public benchmark, profile and source verification

The performance project's baseline (spec WP-00). Every later work package compares against these figures, on the same machine, corpus and toolchain. This report holds the conclusions. The profiler's full output is in the files it links to.

## Reproduction of the existing gates

- `npm test` (native, differential, Wasm, types, package, upstream suite, separators, size budget) passed on this Linux machine at `6e683af`. This is the first Linux run recorded; TODO.md listed Linux as never run.
- `npm run bench:public` ran on the committed tree `1514d7b`: 9 paired rounds after two warm-ups. Before timing, 934,016 result rows were compared with MiniSearch: maximum relative score difference 0.

Compare the speed-ups, not the milliseconds. This Xeon is roughly 2.5× slower than the machine of the 0.12.0 report. The ratios land in the same range, but not within noise of that report:

- **Searches compare better here:** full objects 2.8× against 2.2×.
- **Builds compare worse:** `addAll` 2.5× against 3.3×.

Corrections to the spec's picture:

1. **The facade answers dirty queries exactly, and only dirty ones take the exact entry points.** It enables exact dirty queries (`js/minisearch.js:224`), and `src/lib.rs:1178` routes to the `*_exact` entry points only while `dirt_count > 0`. On a dirty index, a query that meets a stale posting runs twice natively (fused, then the mutating replica), and a boosted query always takes the map path and clears the expansion cache. On a clean index everything takes the fused path.
2. Row tracking is on for every fused full-row search, so WP-02's saving applies to `includeMatch: false` exactly as described. With more than 64 fields, every search pays for a full `MatchInfo`.

## Compatibility findings (callback traces)

`callback_traces.mjs` found two undocumented divergences from MiniSearch at the baseline. Both are recorded as known (the test fails once they are fixed, to have the entry removed):

1. **`search()` reads a stored `score` getter twice per row.** MiniSearch reads it once. This is the WP-01 rerun observed from user code: the discarded sorted run built the rows. A getter whose value changes between reads also gives different scores and order than MiniSearch.
2. **The native `autoSuggest` ignores a stored `score` field.** MiniSearch's `autoSuggest` groups `search()` rows, whose `score` a stored field replaces, so its suggestion scores and order follow the stored values. Here they are the engine's BM25 sums.

Everything else matched MiniSearch: outputs and traces of filters (including score mutation, ties and NaN), boostDocument, boostTerm, prefix/fuzzy callbacks, tokenize/processTerm, throwing callbacks, clean and dirty.

## Matrix

From [the matrix output](2026-10-15-wp00-matrix.md). The release timings come from `pkg/` and the counters and memory from `target/pkg-stats`, both built from the baseline code (the matrix finished before the first Phase 1 build; the "commit" in its header is the HEAD at the time the report was written). 7 to 200 warm samples per cell (1.5 s budget, 4 s at 1M), p50/p95 per call.

Cells: 10k and 100k documents × 1, 4, 16, 65 fields × clean / 50% deleted and vacuumed / 90% discarded and not vacuumed, plus 1M documents × 4 fields clean. 12 query shapes × 4 result shapes per cell.

What it shows:

1. **Latency follows the number of hits, not the corpus size.**
   - A rare term (2, 20 and 200 hits at 10k, 100k and 1M documents) costs 0.08–0.21 ms with `search()` at every size. No hits costs about 0.07 ms: that is the fixed cost of a call.
   - High-hit queries scale linearly. A short prefix at 4 fields (66% of documents) takes 3.7 ms at 10k documents, 69 ms at 100k and 1.08 s at 1M. `searchRaw` takes 1.4 ms, 17 ms and 344 ms.
   - The rows the caller pays for are the cost. That is the case for an opt-in top-K (WP-13) on large corpora, and for cheaper rows in WP-01/02.
2. **Full `search()` costs 2.5–4× `searchRaw` at 1–16 fields, and about 8× at 65 fields** (100k documents, short prefix: 312 ms against 41 ms). With more than 64 fields every search took the map path with full match data, so `includeMatch: false` saved only 20% there (246 ms). PR-006 sends that case to the fused path.
3. **The scratch retains about 190 bytes per document slot** after full-row queries: 18.6 MiB at 100k documents × 4 fields, 184 MiB at 1M. The spec's historical estimate for 1M slots was 60–88 MB. Single-field indexes retain about 125 bytes per slot. Total Rust memory at 1M × 4 fields is 1.06 GiB (Wasm linear memory 1.19 GiB), a quarter of wasm32's 4 GiB ceiling.
4. **Indexing is superlinear at the top:** `addAll` takes 0.34 s at 10k × 4 fields, 4.7 s at 100k and 200 s at 1M (42× for 10× the documents). Not explained yet. Candidates are the expansion-cache invalidation per added document, posting-vector growth, and Wasm memory growth. This is evidence for WP-09/WP-15.
5. **The first call of each query is much slower than its warm calls on large indexes.** Examples: 226 ms against 12 ms (common exact term, 100k × 4); 3.2 s against 0.12 s (AND, 100k × 65); 17.5 s against 0.27 s at 1M. This applies to queries in the middle of a cell too, not only the first of the process. In the 20k profile, first batches and warm batches were within 3%. Under investigation: garbage from the previous query's samples (a major collection of a multi-GiB heap) is the leading suspect. Until it is explained, the matrix's first-call column is not evidence of cold-query cost.
6. **On indexes with tombstones, physical postings far exceed hits.** With 90% discarded and not vacuumed, a common term visits 51,722 postings for 2,465 hits, and every first query materializes its rows twice (fused, then the exact rerun). That is WP-10's tombstone and compaction tradeoff. Vacuuming (the facade compacts afterwards) removes it.

## What the evidence says for the work packages

Bottlenecks by size, at the baseline:

1. **JavaScript row building for full `search()`: about half of its time.** Most of that is the `match` objects (about 130 ms of 165 ms of JS per batch). No work package targets this directly. WP-02 covers the engine half, while the JS half is in `_results`.
2. **Posting traversal and accumulation in the engine:** accumulate is 75–85% of engine time in every search shape. This is WP-05, WP-06, WP-07 and WP-14 territory. The matrix's large cells show how it scales.
3. **Repeated native executions when a stored field shadows `score` (WP-01):** 2–3× the work on those calls, plus a compatibility bug. Without shadowing, the compact calls still pay 15–20% for the stored-score scan.
4. **Dirty-index exact reruns:** up to 2× on first queries, a 99 MiB allocator peak, and Wasm memory that never shrinks. These are separate from WP-01, as the spec says. The cost is in the mutating replica (map-based path), not in the decision to rerun.
5. **Promotion to the JavaScript engine:** 2.2 s for 20,000 documents, and every later call is 3–4× slower. Only query nodes with their own `boostDocument` trigger it in these workloads.

On large corpora (the matrix):

6. **Rows dominate high-hit queries.** Latency grows with the hit count. A UI showing 20 of 660k matches pays for all 660k rows: 1.1 s per `search()` at 1M documents. That is WP-13's case, should such a workload appear (the primary workload, per ADR 0001, is ordinary `search()`).
7. **Indexing at 1M documents is superlinear** (200 s against 4.7 s at 100k), and **the scratch retains about 190 bytes per document slot**. These are WP-09 and WP-07 respectively, scheduled for later phases.

Smaller fixed costs (WP-03, WP-04) are real in the source but small in these batches: plan and expand together are under 2% of engine time with warm caches. Their value is on short queries with few hits, which the matrix measures per call.
